# Decisions

Choices made where the spec (`flange_cutter_prompt.md`) left room, or where the owner changed it.
"Owner" = decided by the project owner; everything else was decided during the build.
"Review" = changed after an independent design review (three reviewers: geometry, printing and
assembly, simplicity) found a concrete failing case.

## Scope and project

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Several cuts in one session are in scope** (owner, overrides spec §7). Cuts may cross at any angle. | Re-importing a half and cutting again makes crossing cuts collide: the new flange grows past the old cut face, can split old bolt holes, and cannot be repeated exactly on the other half. |
| 2 | **Every cut is fully configurable on its own** (owner): plane, flange side, bolt size, hole size, bolt count or spacing, fastening, all flange parameters. A new cut starts as a copy of the selected cut. | Different cuts have different lengths and loads. |
| 3 | License MIT, copyright holder "SilvestrTk" (owner). | Asked as required by spec §9. |
| 4 | The repository stays local; the owner pushes it and checks the first GitHub workflow run (owner). | Pushing is the owner's call. The workflow runs the same `build/build_windows.ps1` that was tested locally. |
| 5 | The user manual gives no material or print-sheet advice (owner, overrides spec §9). | Owner's choice; print orientation and supports are still covered. |
| 6 | Development Python 3.12.10 installed per user with `winget --scope user` (for 1.0.0 also Inno Setup 6.7.3, no longer needed since decision 46). | The development laptop has no admin rights, like the field laptops. |
| 7 | Code size (1.0.1): the Python is about 2,350 lines instead of "about 1,500" (the geometry alone is about 1,900), and the JavaScript about 1,600 (1,500 without blank lines) instead of about 1,200. In 1.0.0 it was about 1,900, 1,500 and 1,600. | Multi-cut (decision 1) roughly doubles the geometry and adds the cut list, per-cut status and cut placement to the page; the owner accepted this when choosing it. The real-mold fixes of 1.0.1 (surface labels, the layered flange and its end cuts, swept bands, the STL clean-up, the launcher's version check) added about 440 lines, 390 of them geometry. |

## Geometry (one cut)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | trimesh's repair steps are called one by one (`update_faces`, `fix_winding`, inversion check). `networkx` is added; `scipy` is dropped. | In trimesh 5, `Trimesh.fix_normals()` silently needs scipy (`body_count`), and `fix_winding` needs networkx. The POC's only scipy use (KD-tree) is replaced by `shapely.STRtree.nearest`. |
| 9 | Tool clearance (rule 8) tests each bolt's tool disc against the **shadow of the mold beyond both flange faces** (one boolean and one projection per cut) instead of a 3D boolean per bolt against the finished halves. | Same test: a vertical cylinder over the tool height hits the mold exactly when its disc hits that shadow (flange, holes and pockets never reach past the flange faces). The 3D solid is built once instead of up to 7 times, and preview and Generate place the same bolts. Verified bolt by bolt against the POC's 3D check. |
| 10 | Only the 2D outline (trimesh `mesh_plane`) shifts the plane by 1 µm when mesh vertices lie exactly on it. Manifold slices and splits at the exact plane. | Vertices on the plane make `mesh_plane` return duplicate edges; moving the 3D split instead left 1 µm slivers that break after float32 STL export. |
| 11 | Flange outlines are **snapped to a 0.01 mm grid** before extruding (review). | Outline vertices made from chain ends lie exactly on the mold surface; Manifold then leaves sub-micron edges that collapse in a float32 STL. The POC itself fails this way at 4 of 5 lengthwise positions; the snap fixed all tested cases (regression tests). |
| 12 | **Leaning walls:** each flange half also loses the POC guard band found at its own outer face (z = ±T). Built as one slab plus trimming prisms that start 0.02 mm off the plane. (The first version also extended guard chains past their ends; removed in decision 43. Since 1.0.1 each half is two layers guarded at both faces, decision 50.) | A straight flange slab next to a leaning wall pokes through into the cavity (3.5 mm³ in a 3-cut test; possible below the 25° warning with M5/M6 flanges on 2 mm walls). For square walls the result is identical to the POC. Two overlapping slabs gave coplanar faces that broke after float32 export, hence the trimming prisms. |
| 13 | For models that are not thin shells (decision 40), **one cavity reference per connected region of the section** (the centroid of that region's convex hull), not one for the whole section (review). | A plane through two separate wall loops (two arms) put the reference between them and grew flange into both cavities. For one region it is the POC rule. |
| 14 | Bolts are shared over stretches by largest remainder, at least 2 per stretch where they fit. A count larger than what fits (spacing ≥ washer + 2 mm) is an error that names the maximum. | The POC's rounding could place fewer bolts than asked; spec §8 wants a clear error for too many bolts. |
| 15 | Bolt distribution per cut: **count** (spec) or **spacing**. New parameters "End distance" (default 3 mm, the POC's fixed value) and "Hole diameter" (empty = ISO hole + extra hole size). | Owner asked for full control of holes and their distribution per cut. |
| 16 | When the wall still blocks tools after moving the bolt line out 6 mm, the layout with the fewest blocked bolts is kept (POC kept the last). | Moving further out without benefit only widens the flange. |
| 17 | Part heights are set from Manifold's double-precision bounding box, not from its float32 mesh output. | Otherwise a tilted part floated 8 µm above z = 0 after export. |
| 18 | Nut-pocket grip = head washer + T + (T − pocket depth) + nut height; the length rule then adds 2 mm. The nut-pocket thickness rule is applied before the layout. | Spec said "compute its own grip"; the layout must use the final T. |
| 19 | Parts list names nuts ISO 4032 and washers ISO 7089. Head height for clearance is taken as the bolt diameter (socket head, ISO 4762). | The spec's data sources; a hex head is lower, so this is on the safe side. |

## Several cuts

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | Every plane cuts the whole model. Each cell of the planes is split into its separate bodies; every body is a printed part, named A, B, C… in order of side signature. With one cut, A is on the normal side, as the spec says. | Simple and predictable; one body per file so each can lie flat (review: a 2-body file left one body in mid-air). |
| 21 | Each cut's section and flange come from the original model only. A bolt position is kept only where its hardware and tool space clear every other cut's flange **and the bolt ends, nuts and heads sticking out of it** (review): `max(0, d − (T + max(H, protrusion))·cos) − r·sin ≥ T_other + protrusion_other + 0.5`, tested every 0.5 mm along the bolt line. | Clearing only the other flange let bolts of crossing cuts run into each other (shown at 90°). One test covers crossing, tilted and parallel cuts, so there is no special case for parallel planes. |
| 22 | Two cuts must either cross inside the wall at 15° or more (warning below 30°), or keep their flanges apart; decided from where each plane lies relative to the other cut's wall section, never from plane origins (review). Parallel cuts too close for tools between them get no bolts, with a warning that names the other cut. At most 6 cuts. | Origin-based distances gave wrong answers for near-parallel planes. |
| 23 | Preview reports errors **per cut** (HTTP 200); Generate refuses while any cut has an error and lists them (review). | One misplaced cut must not blank the preview of the others. |
| 24 | After Generate: loose flange offcuts (no mold inside) are left out with a warning; parts that are mostly flange, parts or joints held by no bolt or only one bolt, flanges that print in mid-air, and parts that are not watertight after export each give a warning. The report lists which parts each cut bolts together (review). | Infinite planes can cut off pieces nobody asked for; the user must see it before printing. |
| 25 | Print orientation: of the cut faces a part has, the one that leaves the **least overhang** goes on the print sheet (ties: the larger face), oriented by the spec's rule for that cut (side A as is, side B turned 180° about the plane x axis) (review). | "Largest face down" printed cross-cut quarters as arches with the cavity overhanging; with one cut there is only one candidate, so the spec's rule is unchanged. |
| 26 | Not done in version 1 (review suggestions): cuts limited to one side of another cut (T-junctions), hexagon rotation for pockets printed sideways, printer bed-size checks. | Kept the app small; listed as ideas in the README. |

## The page

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | The first cut goes across the longest side, through the middle of the model's box; if that plane misses the model (two cups side by side), across the next longest side. | Long molds are mostly cut to fit the printer. |
| 28 | **Add cut** copies the selected cut's settings onto a plane turned 90° (or parallel, a quarter of the model away), choosing one that crosses the model and does not lie on another cut. | The first version stacked new cuts on old ones (review). |
| 29 | **Across X/Y/Z** keep the model's +Z up in the 2D view. **Reset** returns a cut to the plane it started with. Number fields accept a decimal comma. | Predictable views; field users often type commas. |
| 30 | The model is tinted by part before Generate (which side of every cut each point is on). | Users see the parts a cut layout makes while they move the cuts. |
| 31 | Any real change after Generate drops the parts and says "Click Generate to make new parts"; a click on a handle without moving it does not. | Downloads must always match what is shown (review). |
| 32 | **Quit**, **Delete cut** and **Use these settings for all cuts** ask for confirmation. | One misclick must not lose work (review). |
| 33 | The server takes a new free port at every start. When it is gone, the page says to start it again and that it opens a new tab. | Spec: "a free port"; a fixed port can be taken by another program. |
| 34 | The UI test writes the manual's screenshots only with `FLANGECUTTER_SHOTS=1`. | A normal test run must not change files in git (review). |

## Packaging

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 35 | (1.0.0 only, replaced by decision 46.) The installer always installed per user to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\FlangeCutter` and offered no other folder or admin mode. | Spec: never an admin prompt; one known place makes support easier. |
| 36 | (1.0.0 only, replaced by decision 46.) Setup and the uninstaller asked a running Flange Cutter to quit through `/api/shutdown` (port from the lock file). If it did not quit, Setup fell back to Windows Restart Manager and the uninstaller stopped with a message. Uninstall kept `%LOCALAPPDATA%\FlangeCutter`. | Upgrades while the app runs must work; user settings survive. |
| 37 | Windows 10 or later, x64. The exe carries version information and a generated icon (`build/make_icon.py`). | The libraries are 64-bit; version information helps antivirus tools and support. |
| 38 | `build_windows.ps1` signs the exe before zipping, then smoke-tests the exe **unzipped from the ZIP** (start, page, quit), so the shipped exe is the tested one. (1.0.0 tested the build folder before zipping.) Signing in CI only through repository secrets. | A broken or unsigned-by-mistake package must never ship. |
| 39 | The launcher looks for a running app before it loads numpy, trimesh and manifold3d. | A second double-click answers in about 0.5 s instead of several seconds. |
| 46 | **Portable ZIP only, no installer** (owner, 1.0.1). Users extract `FlangeCutter-<version>-portable.zip` by hand and double-click `FlangeCutter.exe`; nothing is installed. Settings and logs stay in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\FlangeCutter`, outside the program folder, so an update is: delete the old folder, extract the new one. When another version still runs, the new exe says so in its console window and closes it when the user presses Enter (`/api/shutdown`), then starts; without a console it opens the running app as before. | Owner's choice: one file to hand out, nothing to install or uninstall. Without Setup nothing closes an old version that still runs, and a second start would silently open the old app. |

## After the first test with real molds (TFO face masks, 2026-11-06)

The owner tested version 1.0.0 with four real face-mask molds (`data/`, kept out of git: they are
made from patient scans). Reported: the flange was sometimes incomplete (especially cutting
vertically through the nose), parts were sometimes invalid, the flange went over the mold's edge
with a rounded end, and rotating a second cut sometimes moved the first.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 40 | **The flange side is decided per surface, not per segment.** At load, every face is labelled with its surface, or rim, or OTHER (`mesh_io.shell_surfaces`, decision 51); "Outside" gives the flange to the outward surfaces for every cut, "Inside" to the inward ones. Models that are not thin shells keep the POC's per-segment rule. | On a face profile (forehead, nose, lips, chin) the POC's rule flipped sides wherever the surface turned toward the reference point: the flange came in 4 to 7 pieces, on both faces, with offcuts and unbolted stretches. Measured on the real molds: now always one flange along one face. |
| 41 | **The flange stops square and flush at the mold's edge** (owner): rim segments get no flange and are guarded like the protected face, and every flange layer is cut flush with the edge at its own heights (decision 47). | The flange used to wrap around the rim with a rounded end, past the mold's edge. |
| 42 | **The flange keeps the POC's 0.6 mm strip into the wall, and fills the wedge where the wall leans away.** On a half where the wall leans away from the flange by more than 8°, that half also gets a band of width W into the wall, which the guards cut 0.1 mm short of the protected face; not within W of an open chain's end. The 0.6 mm is now fixed: the "Overlap into wall" setting is gone (old settings files that contain it still load). | Where the wall leans away from the flange (most oblique cuts of a face), the straight slab touched the wall only near the cut plane and left a wedge-shaped gap: 53 to 89 % of the flange face covered on the real molds in 1.0.0. A strip that deep everywhere would pass the protected face near the cut, hence the two layers per half (decision 50). |
| 43 | Guard chains are no longer extended past their ends for the whole section (part of decision 12, reverted). Ends are handled per chain instead (decision 47). | Where a mold's edge opening was narrower than twice the extension, the two extended ends crossed and the buffer of the self-intersecting line erased a whole flange (0 % flange on a coronal cut of a real mold). |
| 44 | The guard is grown by 0.1 mm: the flange stops 0.1 mm short of the protected face and of the mold's edge. | A flange face lying exactly on a mold face (a square wall, a flat rim) makes slivers that are not watertight after float32 STL export. |
| 45 | **Selecting cuts in the 3D view:** a click on a cut's number badge selects that cut. Near the selected cut's gizmo (within 12 % of the view's smaller side), or where the selected plane is hit within 2 % of the model size of the first plane hit, the selected cut stays selected; anywhere else the plane in front is selected. A press that starts on a gizmo handle is never a click. | Both default cuts go through the model centre, right under the gizmo: a slightly missed handle selected the other cut, and the next rotation moved cut 1. The first fix kept the selected cut wherever its plane lay on the click ray (review: in the default two-cut view the badge of cut 1 no longer worked, and only 24 to 37 % of clicks on its plane did). |
| 47 | **Each flange chain is cut flush with the mold's edge at every height the flange is built for** (z = 0, ±T/2, ±T; `cutter._end_cuts`). At each height: a stretch of the chain with no wall within the flange's reach (W + 2) loses its bands there (the edge is lower), and where a flange chain at that height ends against a guard chain (the rim, or on OTHER models the face where the flange side flips), the guard chain is carried on 3 W + 2 past the end and its band goes too. Each cut trims its own chain only. | Review: the rim guard was only the rim's own strip, so where the face meets the edge off square, or the edge meets the cut at a slant, the flange stood past the edge: up to 12 mm (2,267 mm³) on the test mask, 2,246 to 4,732 mm³ in 1.0.0 with Outside. Now 0 mm³ in all six test cases (sagittal, 30° and 45° cuts, both sides). It also gives OTHER models back 1.0.0's trim at the flip point, and takes the wedge fill off the layers that would lie over a mold's opening near its top. Trimming only its own chain keeps decision 43's failure away. A cut closer to the edge than T/2 or T gets a thinner or shorter flange there (the warnings then name the bolts or parts). |
| 48 | **Open chains are banded by `_swept_band`** (one quad per piece plus joints, round or mitre like GEOS), closed rings by polygon offsets; GEOS single-sided buffers are not used. | On long wavy open chains of a real section (about 500 mm) GEOS returned a band that started W away from the chain (a flange on the wrong side, 101 coronal). On the four real molds GEOS differed from the swept band on 204 of 781 chains, so a checked fallback was not safe either. |
| 49 | The guard band (W + 2) is clipped by the wall section at its height, only its pieces that touch the protected face are kept, and it is built at z = 0, ±T/2 and ±T. | Where the wall bends sharply the band also reaches across the wall and would cut good flange away. |
| 50 | **Each half of the flange is two layers** (0..T/2 and T/2..T; mirrored on side B), each trimmed by the guards at both of its faces. The solid is a core over the cut-face outline from −T/2 to T/2 minus trimming prisms for the inner layers (from 0.02 mm off the plane), plus the outer layers **sitting exactly on the core** at ±T/2. | On a straight wall the protected face comes closest at one face of a layer, so no layer pokes through it, and the outer layer can reach further into a leaning wall than the inner one. Outer layers that overlapped the core by 0.02 mm, or were one grid step smaller, left slivers that were not watertight after export. |
| 51 | **Surfaces by orientation.** Thick patches (area / boundary ≥ the wall thickness estimate) joined across sharp edges are a surface; thin ones joined across sharp edges are a thin region. A surface whose normals point away from its body's centre faces outward. All outward surfaces are "outside" (a raised pad or label on the outer face too); a thin region touching surfaces of one side only belongs to that side (the walls of a pad, an engraved line), one touching both sides is the rim. A body with both sides joined by rim is a thin shell, any other is OTHER. A thin-walled body that comes out OTHER (for example a rounded edge, so its two sides are one surface) gives the warning "Flange Cutter could not find the edge of this mold…". | Review: one "outer" surface per body by best score picked a 12 × 12 mm pad as the outside, and the floor of an engraved line became rim and cut a slot through the flange; choosing the two largest surfaces instead merged the second cavity of two joined cups into the outside. Orientation handles all three, and flat plates and bars (both faces outward) are no longer taken for shells. Without the warning a rounded edge silently fell back to the per-segment rule. |
| 52 | **STL export clean-up** (`mesh_io.stl_bytes`): Manifold simplify (0.1 µm), then the short edge (under 0.1 µm) of float32 needle triangles is merged. Each step is checked on the mesh as an STL reader sees it (float32 points, equal points merged): a step that would leave an open or non-manifold edge is skipped. | Booleans leave slivers that slicers report as errors (12 of 80 real-mold jobs had invalid parts before; 0 after). Review: merging a needle's ends can break the surface when they share another neighbour; a needle is only a warning in a slicer, a broken surface is an error. |
| 53 | **Speed:** the band geometry is simplified by 2 µm before banding (far under the 0.01 mm snap grid), the bands and guards of a cut are built in threads (GEOS releases the GIL), and the flange outline is kept per section, T and W, so a change of the bolts alone reuses it. The chains themselves keep every piece. | The first 1.0.1 geometry made the preview 2 to 4 times slower than 1.0.0. Now the spec's targets hold at 100,000 triangles (preview 0.20 s, Generate 0.66 s, ARCHITECTURE "Performance"); a moved cut still previews about 1.4 to 2 times as long as in 1.0.0, which does less (three section heights, no end cuts), and a change of the bolts alone is faster than in 1.0.0. Threads alone gave 1.5 to 1.9 times on the real molds. Signs and leans are read per piece from the mesh face behind it: a long simplified piece along a thin wall found the other face and put flange in a cup's cavity (critic). |
